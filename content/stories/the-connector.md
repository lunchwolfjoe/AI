## A man whose value is the connection

Arun Agarwal calls himself a connector. Believe him.

Over the past decade, Texas repeatedly placed Agarwal where institutions touched: India-facing commerce and the Governor's Office; private executives and state economic development; Dallas civic boards and billion-dollar capital planning; a private company and a former senior Abbott official; political fundraising and international trade missions.

That is not evidence that Agarwal secretly controlled those institutions. It is evidence of something more revealing about how Texas chose to govern economic development.

**Agarwal is useful because he makes the architecture visible.**

He is not the system. He is what the system rewards: a person whose influence compounds because he can move between organizations that appear separate on an org chart but repeatedly depend on the same relationships in practice.

The distinction matters. A story that tries to turn Agarwal into a mastermind would outrun the evidence. A story that treats him as merely a successful businessman with an unusually full résumé would miss what the public record shows.

**The public record shows reusable access.**

Agarwal's own description of himself captures the function neatly. In an interview reviewed for this investigation, he described himself as born in India, made in Texas, and able to serve both countries he loves. Texas government repeatedly elevated precisely that dual-facing role.

The question is not whether that identity is legitimate. Of course it is.

The question is why so much public and quasi-public authority was repeatedly concentrated in people whose principal value was their ability to connect private networks, political institutions and economic-development machinery — and what rules Texas built to govern the conflicts and dependencies that inevitably follow.

## The résumé is really a map

Agarwal's public roles look eclectic until they are placed in sequence.

He became an Abbott supporter and gubernatorial appointee. He co-chaired Abbott's 2018 economic-development mission to India with Sanjiv Yajnik. He helped build the Indian American CEO Council. By 2022, he was vice chairman of the Texas Economic Development Corporation. In August 2024, Abbott named him chairman.

TxEDC matters because it occupies an unusual space. Abbott's own appointment announcement describes it as the private side of a public-private partnership with the Governor's Economic Development & Tourism Office, charged with marketing Texas domestically and globally as a business destination.

TxEDC does not administer the Texas Enterprise Fund. That distinction is important. State incentive programs run through the Governor's economic-development apparatus. But TxEDC recruits, markets, introduces and helps put prospective investors into the state-development pipeline.

**That makes its chairman neither a ceremonial booster nor a grant officer. The power is relational.**

At the same time, Agarwal was deeply embedded in Dallas civic government. Abbott's 2024 appointment release identified him as president of the Dallas Parks and Recreation Board. Dallas also placed him over the Community Bond Task Force that developed recommendations for the city's 2024 capital-bond package.

The city record is concrete. On December 6, 2023, Agarwal personally briefed the Dallas City Council on the task force's recommendations.

A bond-task-force chair does not award construction contracts. It does not appropriate money. The council and voters retain those powers. But the chair helps organize the recommendations that determine which categories and needs become candidates for public capital.

Agarwal also chaired National Cricket League USA and later disclosed a financial interest when Dallas discussions touched cricket facilities.

These roles are not proof of self-dealing. They are precisely why a public conflict ledger matters.

When the same person moves among economic development, parks, bond planning, private business, sports development, political fundraising and international recruitment, the public should not have to reconstruct the overlaps manually from meeting packets, appointments, company biographies and lobbying records.

## IACEO explains the model

The Indian American CEO Council is one of the clearest windows into Agarwal's theory of influence because he described its purpose more candidly than most organizations describe themselves.

In the interview reviewed for this investigation, Agarwal discussed building recognition of the Indian business community as a source of political and economic power. He compared the ambition to the organized influence achieved by Jewish-American institutions.

The significance is not the ethnic comparison. It is the organizational theory behind it.

Successful executives become more useful when their private stature is aggregated. A CEO by himself has a company. A network of CEOs can host officials, organize delegations, frame policy concerns, recruit investment and become the place politicians call when they need access to a business community.

That is not inherently improper. Chambers of commerce do it every day.

**What makes IACEO relevant is its overlap with Texas's economic-development apparatus.**

Agarwal and Yajnik have occupied leadership roles there. Former Wipro CEO Abidali Neemuchwala later appeared in TxEDC governance; TxEDC's fiscal-year 2020 Form 990 listed him as secretary. A Tech Mahindra executive also appeared on the later IACEO board.

Texas had already spent years recruiting Indian technology companies. Then executives from that world appeared inside institutions helping Texas conduct the next round of recruitment.

Again, the point is not that the recruits captured Texas.

**It is that Texas deliberately built a model in which the line between the business community being recruited and the network doing the recruiting became thin by design.**

## The 2018 mission was an institutional handoff

The personnel surrounding Abbott's first India mission show how the system reproduced itself.

In March 2017, Abbott announced that Robert Allen, then his deputy chief of staff, would leave the Governor's Office to become president and CEO of TxEDC. David Whitley, then Abbott's appointments director, replaced Allen as deputy chief.

A year later, the Governor went to India.

Agarwal and Yajnik co-chaired the mission. Allen was running TxEDC. Whitley traveled with the delegation.

There is nothing mysterious about those facts. They are the ordinary mechanics of a public-private economic-development campaign.

**That ordinariness is exactly why they matter.**

The Governor's Office supplied authority. TxEDC supplied the private-side recruitment structure. Business connectors supplied relationships. Senior officials supplied continuity. The model depended less on formal command than on people who could carry access from one institution into another.

One of the companies Abbott visited was Wipro. The Governor toured its Bengaluru campus and announced a new Wipro Texas Technology Center in Plano.

Years later, Wipro would appear on Whitley's lobbying book.

The relationship had moved from recruitment to representation without ever leaving the same broad political-economic ecosystem.

## Then the private company hired the insider

Agarwal's own private business makes the connector model even easier to see.

Agarwal is associated with Nextt, a Dallas-area company with textile, home-goods and related interests. In April 2020, Dallas ratified an emergency purchase from Nextt for personal protective equipment, with authorization up to $330,000.

An emergency procurement during the first months of COVID-19 is not evidence of favoritism. Cities were buying scarce supplies under extraordinary conditions.

**The more important event came afterward.**

Texas Ethics Commission records show that in 2020, Nextt Affiliated retained David Whitley as a registered lobbyist. The same year's state lobbyist roster also lists Whitley for Wipro and Accenture.

The dates matter. The known Nextt registration begins in June 2020. The Dallas PPE purchase came first.

So the record does not support the claim that Whitley obtained that purchase for Nextt. It supports a different and cleaner statement: a private company associated with Agarwal, already doing business with a public entity, hired a former senior Abbott official whose career had run through gubernatorial appointments, the Governor's executive office, a secretary-of-state tenure and state economic development.

This was not a consultant with a generic Rolodex.

**Whitley's value was knowledge of Texas government and relationships inside it.**

**That is what a revolving door sells.**

## The circle returned to India

In January 2024, Abbott led another economic-development mission to India.

The Governor's public releases identify Agarwal, then TxEDC vice chairman, beside Abbott throughout the trip. TxEDC and the Governor's economic-development office organized the mission, whose stated purpose was to encourage trade, job creation and Indian investment in Texas.

Abbott met leaders from some of the world's largest technology companies. His office highlighted Tata Consultancy Services, HCLTech and other companies in a series of releases from Mumbai and New Delhi.

Agarwal was no longer merely a businessman who knew people in India. He was part of the formal Texas recruitment apparatus.

Contemporaneous delegation material reviewed in this investigation also places Whitley on the 2024 trip with Gregory Strategies and identifies a Nextt representative, Parikshit Dhume, in the delegation.

The investigation has not established that Nextt paid Whitley for that mission or that Whitley attended on Nextt's behalf.

That caveat is important.

But the institutional recurrence remains striking. Agarwal, Whitley and a Nextt representative were again participating in the same Texas-linked India mission years after Nextt had retained Whitley.

TxEDC sponsored the overall mission. Agarwal later became its chairman.

**The relationship did not need to be secret to be valuable. It needed to remain reusable.**

## Dallas shows what reusable access looks like at home

If the state story is about international recruitment, the Dallas story is about the domestic reuse of credibility.

By 2023, Mayor Eric Johnson had put Agarwal in charge of the Community Bond Task Force. Dallas's own records show the task force meeting throughout the year, developing and reconciling recommendations across parks, streets, flood control, critical facilities, economic development, housing and other needs.

On November 4, the task force finalized its recommendations. City minutes identify Agarwal as chair and record him leading the discussion over election timing. In December, he presented the recommendations to the City Council.

The importance is not that he controlled a billion dollars. He did not.

**The importance is that a man whose public value had been built partly through state economic development and private business relationships was trusted to chair another public process allocating political attention across a huge capital program.**

At the same time, he remained president of the Parks Board and chairman of a sports enterprise with interests in Dallas facilities.

That concentration of roles creates predictable questions. Did a capital category overlap with a private or nonprofit interest? Was a recusal required? Were relationships with vendors disclosed? Did an entity invited onto an international mission later seek city business? Did a sports-development proposal touch a board the same person chaired?

Those questions do not imply the answer is yes.

**They reveal why disclosure systems exist.**

The problem is that Dallas and Texas generally publish their records by institution. The Parks Board keeps one file. The bond task force keeps another. TxEDC publishes another. Ethics records sit elsewhere. Corporate relationships sit elsewhere still.

The connector experiences one network.

The public receives six filing cabinets.

## The other end of the bridge changes what 'connector' means

The larger Texans First investigation found something that makes Agarwal's role easier to understand without making it more sinister.

At the other end of Texas's India-facing relationship, Andhra Pradesh built a formal apparatus for converting overseas networks into investment, offices, operations and jobs.

The Andhra Pradesh Non-Resident Telugu Society describes an investment function that helps overseas investors navigate government and establish businesses or offshore operations. Its coordinator structure treats diaspora representatives as economic-development infrastructure.

ITServe member companies participated in that recruitment. Andhra offered the kinds of things economic-development governments normally offer: space, subsidies, workforce support, facilitation and access.

That does not establish that Agarwal controlled APNRTS, ITServe or Andhra policy. This investigation has not found evidence that he did.

**It establishes why a person who can move among Texas political institutions, private executives and India-facing networks becomes strategically valuable.**

The bridge did not require one person to govern both ends.

It required connectors on each end who could translate a relationship into an introduction, an introduction into an institution and an institution into another relationship.

## That is the design

Public-private economic development is built to move faster than ordinary government.

That is part of its appeal. A governor wants business leaders who can make calls. A mayor wants civic leaders who can marshal support. A recruitment corporation wants directors who understand corporate decision-makers. Companies want advisers who understand government. Diaspora organizations want members who can translate between markets.

Put those incentives together and the connector becomes the highest-value person in the system.

**The same feature that makes the model efficient also creates its governance risk.**

Relationships accumulate faster than disclosure rules.

A person's previous role becomes the reason for the next appointment. The appointment becomes proof of stature. The stature becomes business access. Business access creates new civic roles. Civic roles create still more proximity to government.

No corrupt bargain is required.

**Influence compounds on its own.**

## The records Texas should keep

The answer is not to prohibit business leaders from public service or former officials from working in the private sector.

**It is to make the overlaps legible.**

TxEDC should publish investor tiers, annual contributions, mission sponsors, delegate rosters, mission costs, corporate referrals, conflict disclosures and recusals in one searchable place.

When its directors or officers have private interests in companies connected to a mission, prospect or state incentive pipeline, that relationship should be disclosed before the meeting rather than reconstructed years later.

Dallas should do the same for high-level advisory bodies. Bond-task-force members should have a public conflict file showing relevant private businesses, nonprofit leadership, development interests and recusals tied to categories under consideration.

Former senior state officials representing companies that sell to Texas should be traceable through a simple revolving-door register connecting prior office, current client, subject matter and relevant state contracts.

And economic-development claims should be followed beyond the announcement: promised jobs, verified jobs, state support, subsequent amendments and — where the work is performed through contractors — the actual geography of taxpayer-funded labor.

**Those are not punishment mechanisms.**

**They are the accounting controls a relationship-driven system requires.**

## Agarwal is not the scandal

Arun Agarwal did not invent Texas's public-private model.

The Governor appointed him because he was good at it.

Dallas trusted him because he was good at it.

Business organizations elevated him because he was good at it.

His influence is not difficult to explain once the institutions are viewed together.

**He could connect them.**

That is why the most important conclusion about Agarwal is also the least sensational.

He is not evidence that one man captured Texas.

**He is evidence that Texas built a system in which connectivity itself becomes a form of public power — while the public records needed to audit that power remain scattered across the institutions the connector moves between.**

Agarwal is not the scandal.

**He is the design.**
