The revolving-door evidence is strongest when presented as a disclosure/governance problem rather than an accusation of contract steering.

## David Whitley

Whitley served Abbott as appointments director and deputy chief of staff, later became secretary of state and participated in Abbott's 2018 India mission. Texas Ethics Commission primary records show that in 2020 he represented Wipro beginning February 8 and Accenture and Nextt beginning June 26.

The combination is institutionally important because Wipro was a company Abbott courted on the 2018 trip, Accenture is a major government technology contractor, and Nextt is associated with Arun Agarwal, a prominent India-facing Texas economic-development connector.

It does not prove Whitley caused any specific contract or purchase. The known Nextt registration also postdates Dallas's emergency PPE purchase, so that purchase cannot be attributed to his lobbying on the current record.

## John B. Scott

Abbott appointed Scott presiding officer of DIR in 2016. A later Governor's biography says Scott developed technology/security strategy for executive agencies and boards. Primary Texas Ethics records show Scott began representing Deloitte in January 2024 and continued in later cycles. Deloitte holds a broad current DIR portfolio, including ITSAC DIR-CPO-5691 beginning in February 2025.

This creates a clean revolving-door fact pattern: a former DIR presiding officer later represents a company selling extensively through DIR. It does not establish that Scott influenced an award.

## Ruth Ruggero Hughs

The latest intermediary census identifies Hughs — former TWC chair and Texas secretary of state — as another former senior Abbott official later representing a technology vendor, Risun Solutions, which appears in the DIR market. This item should remain in the evidence report as a verified ledger finding but should receive a fresh primary Texas Ethics Commission preservation pass before story publication.

## Jerry Strickland and Cognizant

Abbott's own records describe Strickland as an adviser/spokesman dating to 2003 and later head of the Texas Office of State-Federal Relations. Texas Ethics records show Cognizant retaining him in 2024 and again in 2025.

Cognizant's separate federal lobbying operation in 2025 disclosed work on H-1B/high-skilled immigration, U.S.-India bilateral issues, workforce/STEM, AI, privacy and tax. The federal and Texas lobbying programs must not be conflated without evidence of coordination.

## What This Means

These are lawful relationships unless evidence proves otherwise. The significance is institutional: people who held senior roles in Abbott's administration later represented major technology vendors participating in the same public contracting ecosystem they once oversaw.

The relevant question is not whether these relationships are illegal. It is whether the public contracting system provides adequate visibility into the networks connecting policy-makers, vendors and the labor actually performing taxpayer-funded work.
