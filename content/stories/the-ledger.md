This investigation began with a narrow question: after Gov. Greg Abbott declared that taxpayer-funded employment opportunities should go to Texans first, could Texas show who actually performed the technology work it bought through private contractors?

The answer, as documented across five preceding chapters, is incomplete.

## What the Investigation Proves

| Classification | Finding |
|---------------|---------|
| **Proven** | Texas deliberately cultivated India-facing economic-development relationships under Abbott through missions, corporate recruitment and a public-private TxEDC/Governor structure. |
| **Proven** | Abbott's own later profile reports larger Texas-company investment/job announcements into India than Indian-company announcements into Texas over 2015–24. |
| **Proven** | Andhra built explicit government-linked machinery for NRT investment and offshore operations and historically recruited ITServe-linked companies with office/incentive/workforce support. |
| **Proven** | Texas maintains a large private-vendor technology staffing market through DIR and agencies. |
| **Proven** | The standard current ITSAC templates reviewed do not themselves provide a dedicated statewide worker-immigration/offshore-location reporting field. |
| **Proven** | Some agencies add Texas-location requirements, demonstrating that worker geography can be controlled when requested. |
| **Proven** | Former senior Abbott officials later represented technology companies participating around the public technology/economic-development ecosystem. |
| **Proven** | Texas audit and Sunset records document recurring contracting-governance concerns alongside later improvements. |

## What the Investigation Does NOT Prove

| Classification | Finding |
|---------------|---------|
| **Not yet proven** | That a particular Texas-funded technology task was performed offshore through the Andhra machinery documented here. |
| **Not yet proven** | That a specific H-1B worker filled the Digerati/TWC assignment described in the series. |
| **Not yet proven** | That Abbott personally knew of APNRTS/DTC or intended contractor labor to fall outside his Texans-first policy. |
| **Not yet proven** | That any lobbying relationship or campaign contribution caused a procurement, grant, mission decision or immigration policy outcome. |

## The Central Finding

The evidence supports a public-policy mismatch: Texas leaders made geographically specific promises about jobs while the state's public contracting systems largely account at the vendor/contract level.

**The central proven failure is measurement, not a proven conspiracy.**

## Texas Can Change Policy When It Wants To

Current 2026 ITSAC pages show amendments implementing Abbott's Executive Order GA-48 restrictions concerning entities tied to specified foreign-adversary countries. India is not one of the countries targeted by that order.

The comparison is administrative, not geopolitical: **Texas demonstrated that it can translate gubernatorial policy into statewide contract amendments.** The record reviewed for this report has not identified an equivalent post-Jan. 2026 statewide ITSAC amendment requiring worker-level reporting for Texas residency, work location, temporary-visa category or offshore delivery.

## The Proposed Reform

The strongest defensible demand is therefore a statewide labor ledger capable of measuring:

| Field | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| **Agency** | Which public entity purchased the labor |
| **Contract** | Which vehicle authorized the purchase |
| **Prime Vendor** | Which company holds the contract |
| **Subcontractor** | Who actually employs the worker (if different) |
| **Labor Category** | What type of work |
| **TX Resident (Y/N)** | Does the worker live in Texas |
| **Work Geography** | Where is the work physically performed |
| **Authorization Category** | Aggregate visa/work-authorization status |
| **Hours** | How much labor |
| **Dollars** | How much payment |

**No names. No individual PII. Just the aggregate accounting that would let Texas answer its own promise.**

## The Accountability Questions

These questions remain open:

1. Can Texas produce a statewide count of contractor labor hours by Texas residency, actual work location, employer/subcontractor and temporary-work-visa category?

2. What is the legal and administrative scope of Abbott's Jan. 27, 2026 directive as applied to private contractors and subcontractors performing taxpayer-funded work?

3. Did DIR, TWC or another agency issue post-directive guidance to ITSAC vendors concerning H-1B workers, Texas residency or offshore delivery?

4. What were the exact firm-level disbursements under the 2016–18 Andhra/APNRT/ITServe recruitment programs?

5. Did any Texas public contract or task order use personnel physically located at a documented Andhra delivery center?

## The Bottom Line

The investigation should explicitly state that India, Indian companies, diaspora organizations, ITServe members and H-1B workers are not treated as a single category. Economic development abroad, lawful immigration sponsorship, lobbying, litigation, donations and civic participation are not wrongdoing.

**The central accountability question is whether Texas made a promise it built no consistent public accounting system to measure.**

The answer appears to be yes.

The fix is not to stop economic development or immigration. The fix is to measure what Texas promised to prioritize — and show the public the results.

*Show us the ledger.*
