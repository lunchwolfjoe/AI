The cleanest quantitative description of the Texas–India bridge comes from the Governor's own economic-development office. Its July 2025 Texas & India profile covers January 2015 through December 2024 and reports both directions of announced investment.

## The Numbers

| Direction | Projects | Announced Capital | Announced Jobs |
|-----------|----------|-------------------|----------------|
| Indian companies → Texas | 74 | $2.7 billion | >8,650 |
| Texas companies → India | 175 | $7.6 billion | >59,300 |
| **Ratio (TX→India / India→TX)** | **2.36×** | **2.81×** | **6.86×** |

*Source: Governor's Economic Development & Tourism Office, Texas & India profile, July 2025.*

## What These Figures Mean

The figures are important because they define the relationship using Texas's own promotional accounting. They show more Texas-company projects, substantially more announced Texas-company capital and far more announced jobs in India than the reverse flow reported in Texas.

The figures cannot be converted into 'jobs shipped from Texas.' A new job created in India may support a new market, product or delivery capability rather than replace an existing Texas worker. The report therefore uses them as evidence of institutional asymmetry and scale, not displacement.

## The Job-Count Discrepancy

Abbott's 2024 India-mission materials repeatedly used a different inbound figure: 59 Indian-company projects, $1.4 billion and more than 10,300 Texas jobs, citing Indian industry estimates. The later profile reports more projects and capital but fewer jobs.

This is a fact-checking issue, not yet a finding of manipulation. The source, methodology, date range or project definitions may differ. Any final publication should ask the Governor's Office to reconcile the two figures before characterizing the change.

## Texas Built a Public-Private Bridge

The relationship was not merely the aggregate behavior of private companies. Texas actively cultivated it.

Abbott's 2018 India trip included visits to Wipro's Electronic City facility and Infosys headquarters. At Wipro, Abbott joined executives to announce a new Plano technology center. The 2024 mission again visited major corporate facilities, including TCS, HCLTech and other business targets.

TxEDC operates as the privately funded marketing/recruitment side of a public-private arrangement with the Governor's Economic Development & Tourism Office. The investigation corrected an early overstatement: TxEDC does not administer the Texas Enterprise Fund. State economic-development offices administer those incentives.

## Personnel Continuity

Abbott's March 2017 staff announcement placed the public-private continuity in plain view: deputy chief of staff Robert Allen left the Governor's Office to become president and CEO of TxEDC, with the announcement stating that he would continue working with the Governor to attract businesses to Texas. David Whitley moved into the deputy-chief role.

This is not evidence of wrongdoing; it is evidence of institutional continuity.

Later, Arun Agarwal moved from mission and civic roles into the chairmanship of TxEDC. His significance is best understood as a connector case study: the same individual could participate in India missions, public-private economic development, Dallas civic structures and private business without one public system showing the complete relationship map.
