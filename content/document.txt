TEXANS FIRST*
EVIDENCE REPORT
A findings-based synthesis of the Texas–India economic-development, public technology contracting, labor-visibility, intermediary and Andhra evidence
Version 1  |  September 1, 2026  |  Prepared from the master investigative ledger


Status: working investigative report. This document distinguishes established facts from derived findings, contextual evidence, open questions and unsupported inferences. It is not a substitute for the underlying source records.
Executive findings
This investigation began with a narrow question: after Gov. Greg Abbott declared that taxpayer-funded employment opportunities should go to Texans first, could Texas show who actually performed the technology work it bought through private contractors?
The record assembled to date shows that the answer is incomplete. Texas maintains extensive records of vendors, statewide contracts, purchase orders, job categories, rates and payments. It does not appear to maintain one public statewide ledger that consistently ties those purchases to the residence, work location, immigration/work-authorization category and subcontracting status of the individual workers performing the labor.
That accounting gap became significant only because Texas simultaneously spent a decade building an unusually deep India-facing economic-development relationship, while governments and industry organizations on the other side of that relationship built explicit machinery to attract investment, offshore operations, technical work and jobs.
1. Abbott's own bilateral figures show a highly asymmetric announced investment relationship.
The Governor's Economic Development & Tourism Office reports 74 Indian-company projects in Texas from 2015–2024, $2.7 billion in announced capital and more than 8,650 announced jobs. Over the same period, Texas companies announced 175 projects in India, $7.6 billion in capital and more than 59,300 jobs. These are reported/announced FDI figures, not a job-loss ledger. EV-2026-0901-129, -130.
2. Texas deliberately built the relationship through public-private economic development.
Abbott led India missions, toured major technology-company facilities and used TxEDC alongside the Governor's economic-development office to recruit investment. Personnel moved between the Governor's Office and the private-side economic-development corporation. The evidence supports deliberate bridge-building, not a claim that TxEDC itself administers state incentive grants.
3. The contractor workforce is more difficult to see than the direct state workforce.
Abbott's Jan. 27, 2026 H-1B directive imposes petition/reporting controls on state agencies and public universities. Current DIR ITSAC contracting continues to provide private-vendor technology labor through requests for resumes and statements of work. Standard current templates reviewed identify remote work and staffing categories but contain no dedicated statewide field for citizenship, work-authorization category or offshore delivery. EV-2026-0901-151 through -160.
4. Agency-specific controls prove Texas can ask more precise questions.
Current HHSC solicitations reproduced by multiple vendors require Texas residence or Austin-area proximity for certain positions. One recruiter adds a 'No H1B' restriction, but the original agency solicitation has not been obtained, so that language cannot be attributed to HHSC. EV-2026-0901-161 through -163.
5. Andhra Pradesh built explicit state machinery to capture overseas investment, operations and jobs.
Official APNRTS materials describe government-assisted investment pipelines and an Investments Wing that facilitates NRT investors establishing MSMEs or 'offshore operations in any segment.' Coordinator rules require investment promotion. Contemporaneous reports document office, rent, hiring, training and government-access incentives offered around ITServe recruitment. EV-2026-0901-132 through -136 and earlier APNRT evidence.
6. ITServe is best understood as an organized industry institution, not as a synonym for any nationality or visa category.
Its network joined business education, lobbying, litigation, political access and international economic-development activity. Historical APNRT/ITServe evidence shows the association functioning as a recruitment channel for Andhra operations. Membership alone does not prove H-1B dependence, offshoring or Texas public-contract participation.
7. Texas's governing network includes a documented revolving door.
Former senior Abbott officials later represented technology vendors or companies around the same ecosystem. Primary records establish David Whitley's 2020 client roster including Wipro, Accenture and Nextt; John B. Scott's later Deloitte representation after he chaired DIR; and Jerry Strickland's Cognizant registrations after a long Abbott relationship. These are lawful relationships unless evidence proves otherwise. EV-2026-0901-108 through -128.
8. Auditors repeatedly identified contracting and oversight weaknesses, while later reviews also credit DIR with improvement.
The 2016 audit found contractor-management and E-Verify verification weaknesses and incomplete implementation of earlier DIR recommendations. A 2021 audit found a High-rated vendor-scoring/transparency issue. Sunset later credited improvement but identified remaining procurement and pricing issues. In April 2025, 9 of the 25 largest agencies warranted added monitoring; by June 2026, 13 of 25 did. EV-2026-0901-137 through -150.
9. Several company case studies join the Texas public market to India-side operating capacity, but the final transaction chain remains open.
Amzur is a strong example: a Texas ITSAC vendor with Texas public staffing activity and documented Andhra/offshore capability. Dallas Technology Centre is another: an ITServe-linked Andhra development approved for state industrial land and later used in a TCS expansion. These facts do not yet prove that a Texas-funded task was delivered offshore.
10. The central proven failure is measurement, not a proven conspiracy.
The evidence supports a public-policy mismatch: Texas leaders made geographically specific promises about jobs while the state's public contracting systems largely account at the vendor/contract level. The strongest defensible demand is therefore a statewide labor ledger capable of measuring Texas residency, actual work location, subcontracting, temporary-visa use in aggregate, hours and dollars.
Methodology
Source hierarchy
The project uses a deliberately conservative evidence hierarchy. Primary government records, executed contracts, official audits, campaign/lobbying filings, IRS filings, corporate records and first-party documents receive the greatest weight. Contemporaneous reporting is used when it quotes principals or describes events for which the primary document has not yet been recovered. Secondary aggregators are used as leads or corroboration unless independently matched to primary records.
Core evidentiary rules
Term / record
	What this report permits
	LCA
	A Labor Condition Application is employer-side labor/visa process evidence. It is not a visa approval and not proof that a named worker performed a particular Texas contract.
	DIR contract
	A statewide master contract establishes eligibility to sell under the vehicle. It is not evidence that an agency bought anything.
	Purchase order
	A purchase order establishes authorization up to the stated scope/amount. It is not always evidence of the amount ultimately paid.
	Payment
	A payment proves public money moved to the vendor, but not who performed the labor or where.
	Remote work
	Remote does not mean offshore.
	Corporate India presence
	An India office or offshore-delivery capability does not prove a Texas-funded task was delivered there.
	ITServe membership
	Membership is evidence of association participation, not a visa category, nationality, procurement outcome or improper coordination.
	Lobbying
	A registration establishes a disclosed representation relationship. Prospective compensation is not proof of actual payment; representation is not proof of influence.
	Donation
	A lawful contribution establishes political support/access context, not a purchased official act.
	FDI
	Announced/reported capital and jobs are not audited realized payroll, net welfare or a direct measure of Texas jobs lost.
	Audit distribution
	A report sent to an office does not prove a named official personally read it.
	MOU/incentive announcement
	A memorandum, advertised subsidy or announced benefit is not proof of disbursement.
	

Analytical method
The investigation joins records across entities and time rather than treating any one database as self-explanatory. The preferred join keys are legal entity name, taxpayer/vendor identifier, contract number, solicitation number, LCA case number, lobbying client/lobbyist identity, corporate registration and named public role. When names are ambiguous, the record is held as a lead rather than treated as a match.
The master ledger also preserves corrections. That matters because several early theories were weakened or changed by later records. For example, Whitley's Accenture representation began earlier than initially believed; Amzur's India presence predates the 2016 Andhra recruitment push; and the 2024 versus 2025 Governor's Office Texas-job estimates use different source bases and should not be called a contradiction until methodologies are reconciled.
Finding I — The bilateral investment relationship
The cleanest quantitative description of the Texas–India bridge comes from the Governor's own economic-development office. Its July 2025 Texas & India profile covers January 2015 through December 2024 and reports both directions of announced investment.

The figures are important because they define the relationship using Texas's own promotional accounting. They show more Texas-company projects, substantially more announced Texas-company capital and far more announced jobs in India than the reverse flow reported in Texas.
The figures cannot be converted into 'jobs shipped from Texas.' A new job created in India may support a new market, product or delivery capability rather than replace an existing Texas worker. The report therefore uses them as evidence of institutional asymmetry and scale, not displacement.
The job-count discrepancy
Abbott's 2024 India-mission materials repeatedly used a different inbound figure: 59 Indian-company projects, $1.4 billion and more than 10,300 Texas jobs, citing Indian industry estimates. The later profile reports more projects and capital but fewer jobs. EV-2026-0901-131.
This is a fact-checking issue, not yet a finding of manipulation. The source, methodology, date range or project definitions may differ. Any final publication should ask the Governor's Office to reconcile the two figures before characterizing the change.
Finding II — Texas built a public-private bridge
The relationship was not merely the aggregate behavior of private companies. Texas actively cultivated it.
Abbott's 2018 India trip included visits to Wipro's Electronic City facility and Infosys headquarters. At Wipro, Abbott joined executives to announce a new Plano technology center. The 2024 mission again visited major corporate facilities, including TCS, HCLTech and other business targets. The official itineraries reviewed do not show Abbott traveling to Andhra Pradesh, APNRTS or Dallas Technology Centre.
TxEDC operates as the privately funded marketing/recruitment side of a public-private arrangement with the Governor's Economic Development & Tourism Office. The investigation corrected an early overstatement: TxEDC does not administer the Texas Enterprise Fund. State economic-development offices administer those incentives.
Personnel continuity
Abbott's March 2017 staff announcement placed the public-private continuity in plain view: deputy chief of staff Robert Allen left the Governor's Office to become president and CEO of TxEDC, with the announcement stating that he would continue working with the Governor to attract businesses to Texas. David Whitley moved into the deputy-chief role. This is not evidence of wrongdoing; it is evidence of institutional continuity.
Later, Arun Agarwal moved from mission and civic roles into the chairmanship of TxEDC. His significance is best understood as a connector case study: the same individual could participate in India missions, public-private economic development, Dallas civic structures and private business without one public system showing the complete relationship map.
Finding III — The public technology market sees vendors better than workers
Texas's technology contracting architecture is extensive and sophisticated at the vendor level. DIR maintains statewide purchasing vehicles, approved vendor lists, standard solicitations, staff categories, not-to-exceed rates, HUB subcontracting plans and statement-of-work review processes.
Current DIR materials state that ITSAC customers may competitively solicit through requests for resumes or SOWs. For state-agency technology SOWs over $50,000, DIR reviews the SOW before solicitation and reviews/signs the final SOW before it becomes valid and money is paid. EV-2026-0901-151 and -160.
What current standard templates capture
The current-generation Appendix E staffing template asks whether all work is expected to be remote and asks what workspace/equipment the agency and vendor will provide. It contains cybersecurity obligations for vendor employees, officers and subcontractors accessing state systems. EV-2026-0901-154.
In the two current templates independently reviewed for this project, no dedicated field was found for citizenship, immigration/work-authorization category or offshore delivery. The staffing table focuses on labor category, title/level, estimated hours and rate. EV-2026-0901-155.
The underlying RFO states that hourly bill rates include wages, benefits, fees, usual living expenses and commuting to the customer's designated primary work site. That shows the procurement model can recognize a worksite, but it does not by itself create public reporting of each remote worker's actual geography. EV-2026-0901-157.
Agency-specific controls
The evidence also contains an important qualification. Current 2026 HHSC solicitations reproduced by multiple staffing vendors impose Texas-locality requirements for some positions. One hybrid Software Engineer 3 request requires the candidate to be local to the Austin area; another remote PeopleSoft request is consistently reproduced as allowing candidates anywhere within Texas. EV-2026-0901-161 and -162.
A Pedigo Staffing posting for the PeopleSoft solicitation says candidates must be U.S. citizens, green-card holders or EAD holders and states 'No H1B.' That is presently a vendor/recruiter record, not a primary HHSC solicitation. It should not be attributed to HHSC until the original is obtained. EV-2026-0901-163.
The existence of agency-specific controls strengthens the governance question: if worker geography is material enough to be specified in individual solicitations, why is it not standardized into a public statewide labor ledger?
Case study — Digerati and the Texas Workforce Commission
Digerati Systems illustrates why company-level records cannot answer a worker-level policy promise.
The company's current Texas procurement footprint includes ITSAC and DBITS contract vehicles. The internal Digerati case file also identifies a TWC staff-augmentation award with a $363,377 ceiling dated February 6, 2026, shortly after Abbott's H-1B directive. The project notes identify the role as Business Analyst 2 work supporting unemployment-insurance data exchange.
The working case file separately records certified LCAs naming Texas DIR and TxDOT as secondary entities in earlier periods. Those LCAs do not establish that the workers started, were paid through the identified public contract or remained on those assignments.
The project has also preserved evidence of India-based Digerati recruiters sourcing Texas public-agency roles, including TWC-style requirements. India-based recruiting is lawful and does not mean the work itself was performed in India; several of the postings are Texas-local or Texas-telework roles.
The case therefore demonstrates an information asymmetry rather than an H-1B finding. Public records can identify a vendor, category, rate, hours and payment. They do not necessarily tell the public whether the individual worker was a Texas resident or what work-authorization category applied.
Before publication, the TWC SOW, invoices and primary Comptroller transaction export should be preserved and the final payment total updated.
Finding IV — Andhra built machinery to capture the other side
Andhra Pradesh is the strongest evidence that the Texas relationship had an institutionally organized receiving end.
Official APNRTS materials describe the organization as a government-linked nodal body serving non-resident Telugus and investment. Its current investment portal offers government introductions, policy/incentive matching, site selection, land support, approval facilitation, implementation tracking and aftercare. EV-2026-0901-132.
A government district page describing APNRTS says its Investments Wing facilitates NRT investors to establish MSMEs or 'offshore operations in any segment' under Andhra policies. Coordinator rules require promoting Andhra investment and include bringing prospective investors and closing investment deals. EV-2026-0901-133.
The current language should not be projected backward automatically. But contemporaneous 2016–18 reporting documents a matching historical program: ITServe-member recruitment, plug-and-play offices, rent support, per-hire subsidy, workforce training, policy changes and access to state opportunities.
Scale
Contemporaneous records in the master ledger report 32 IT companies established with approximately 3,090 jobs by September 2017, 21 more planned, 54 firms facilitated within roughly 16 months by January 2018, and a broader target to accelerate the program. These figures are contemporaneous program claims and still require firm-by-firm validation of operations, employment and subsidy receipt.
The institutional asymmetry
Texas promotional records emphasize projects announced into Texas. Andhra's machinery went further down the operating stack: land, office capacity, incentives, training, labor matching and implementation support. This is ordinary economic development viewed from the receiving side.
The relevant Texas question is not whether Andhra was wrong to compete for jobs. It is whether Texas created an equivalent public accounting system capable of showing how much taxpayer-funded labor and value remained in Texas while the same business networks expanded abroad.
Case study — Dallas Technology Centre
APIIC's September 2015 official minutes approved Dallas Technology Centre for a plot at IT-NONSEZ Hill 2 in Visakhapatnam. Later contemporaneous reporting described DTC as a large project associated with ITServe-linked companies and named planned occupants including IntelliSoft, Primus and Kairos. EV-2026-0901-135.
The records presently support the existence of a Texas-branded, ITServe-linked commercial technology real-estate platform receiving state industrial land. They do not yet establish the final contribution percentages of all partners, exact subsidy receipts or that every planned tenant occupied the property.
The project's later life is significant. Andhra later approved DTC to sublease premises to TCS and recommended a special package around TCS operations. A separate 2025 Andhra action allocated additional Visakhapatnam land to TCS at a symbolic price. These are separate transactions and must not be conflated. EV-2026-0901-136.
No evidence reviewed establishes that Abbott or Texas caused the DTC-to-TCS arrangement. Its significance is continuity: infrastructure seeded in the earlier recruitment era remained part of Andhra's technology-investment platform years later.
Case study — Amzur
Amzur is one of the strongest firm-level joins because the same business group spans Texas public technology staffing and Andhra/offshore operating capacity.
DIR awarded Amzur an ITSAC contract beginning in August 2016. The master ledger also contains later Texas public staff-augmentation activity and a named HHSC Enterprise Architect purchase. At the same time, Amzur appears in the Andhra/ITServe recruitment record and today markets offshore delivery with a Visakhapatnam presence.
A correction is essential: Amzur Technologies Private Limited existed in Andhra from 2006, long before the 2016 recruitment push. A separate Amzur Infotech Private Limited was incorporated in November 2016. APNRT therefore cannot be described as creating Amzur's entire Indian capability.
The open transaction question is narrower and stronger: what incremental Andhra benefits, office support or hiring incentives did the 2016 program provide, and did any Texas taxpayer-funded assignment use that delivery capacity? No current evidence closes that loop.
Finding V — ITServe organized a fragmented industry
ITServe's significance is not that every member behaves the same way. It is that the association gave a fragmented consulting/staffing industry institutional capacity.
The evidence documents the association at the intersection of business education, political advocacy, high-skilled-immigration litigation, member networking and international recruitment. Contemporaneous APNRT/ITServe reporting quotes principals describing government engagement for member benefits and Andhra policy accommodations for companies considering operations.
Historical IRS records place Satish Manduva and Sudhakar Pennam on ITServe's governing board in the same broad period that the Andhra recruitment network was operating. EV-2026-0901-003 through -007.
This does not establish that ITServe controlled APNRTS or that every member participated in Andhra recruitment. It establishes an organized channel between an industry association and an overseas economic-development program.
Political activity
The project also preserves a 2022 Abbott/ITServe political cluster: Abbott appeared as chief guest at a Dallas-area ITServe event, and a short period of campaign-finance activity included eight North Texas technology executives giving equal-sized contributions. The payments were lawful. Before final publication, the event record and each contribution should be rechecked directly against primary Texas campaign-finance records.
The evidentiary purpose is not quid pro quo. It is network visibility: people who appear separately as executives, employers, donors, association participants and vendors can be part of a common organized commercial community.
Finding VI — The revolving door governs the bridge
The revolving-door evidence is strongest when presented as a disclosure/governance problem rather than an accusation of contract steering.
David Whitley
Whitley served Abbott as appointments director and deputy chief of staff, later became secretary of state and participated in Abbott's 2018 India mission. Texas Ethics Commission primary records show that in 2020 he represented Wipro beginning February 8 and Accenture and Nextt beginning June 26. EV-2026-0901-114 and -124.
The combination is institutionally important because Wipro was a company Abbott courted on the 2018 trip, Accenture is a major government technology contractor, and Nextt is associated with Arun Agarwal, a prominent India-facing Texas economic-development connector.
It does not prove Whitley caused any specific contract or purchase. The known Nextt registration also postdates Dallas's emergency PPE purchase, so that purchase cannot be attributed to his lobbying on the current record.
John B. Scott
Abbott appointed Scott presiding officer of DIR in 2016. A later Governor's biography says Scott developed technology/security strategy for executive agencies and boards. Primary Texas Ethics records show Scott began representing Deloitte in January 2024 and continued in later cycles. Deloitte holds a broad current DIR portfolio, including ITSAC DIR-CPO-5691 beginning in February 2025. EV-2026-0901-121 through -126.
This creates a clean revolving-door fact pattern: a former DIR presiding officer later represents a company selling extensively through DIR. It does not establish that Scott influenced an award.
Ruth Ruggero Hughs
The latest intermediary census identifies Hughs — former TWC chair and Texas secretary of state — as another former senior Abbott official later representing a technology vendor, Risun Solutions, which appears in the DIR market. This item should remain in the evidence report as a verified ledger finding but should receive a fresh primary Texas Ethics Commission preservation pass before story publication.
Jerry Strickland and Cognizant
Abbott's own records describe Strickland as an adviser/spokesman dating to 2003 and later head of the Texas Office of State-Federal Relations. Texas Ethics records show Cognizant retaining him in 2024 and again in 2025. EV-2026-0901-108 through -110.
Cognizant's separate federal lobbying operation in 2025 disclosed work on H-1B/high-skilled immigration, U.S.-India bilateral issues, workforce/STEM, AI, privacy and tax. EV-2026-0901-111. The federal and Texas lobbying programs must not be conflated without evidence of coordination.

Finding VIII — Texas can impose targeted foreign-related contract rules
Current 2026 ITSAC pages show amendments implementing Abbott's Executive Order GA-48 restrictions concerning entities tied to specified foreign-adversary countries. India is not one of the countries targeted by that order. EV-2026-0901-158.
The comparison is administrative, not geopolitical: Texas demonstrated that it can translate gubernatorial policy into statewide contract amendments. The record reviewed for this report has not identified an equivalent post-Jan. 2026 statewide ITSAC amendment requiring worker-level reporting for Texas residency, work location, temporary-visa category or offshore delivery.
If another statewide mechanism exists, it should be identified by DIR or the Governor's Office during the response process.

Unresolved questions that could materially change the story
• Can Texas produce a statewide count of contractor labor hours by Texas residency, actual work location, employer/subcontractor and temporary-work-visa category?
• What is the legal and administrative scope of Abbott's Jan. 27, 2026 directive as applied to private contractors and subcontractors performing taxpayer-funded work?
• Did DIR, TWC or another agency issue post-directive guidance to ITSAC vendors concerning H-1B workers, Texas residency or offshore delivery?
• What were the exact firm-level disbursements under the 2016–18 Andhra/APNRT/ITServe recruitment programs?
• Did any Texas public contract or task order use personnel physically located at a documented Andhra delivery center?
• What are the executed lease, partner, land-allotment and special-package records for Dallas Technology Centre and TCS?
• Why do Abbott's 2024 and 2025 India-related Texas-job figures differ, and which methodology does the Governor's Office consider authoritative?
• What communications exist between Abbott/TxEDC and Wipro, Infosys, TCS, Cognizant, HCLTech and other named companies around missions, incentives and post-mission follow-up?
• What contacts did Whitley, Scott, Hughs and Strickland make on behalf of the relevant technology clients, and which state agencies were contacted?
• What portion of the DIR/ITSAC vendor market is ITServe-linked or heavily reliant on H-1B labor once entity matching, actual spend and denominators are independently audited?

Publication posture
The most defensible public framing remains institutional rather than ethnic or conspiratorial. The record supports a story about economic-development policy, public contracting, labor visibility, organized industry advocacy and revolving-door governance.
The investigation should explicitly state that India, Indian companies, diaspora organizations, ITServe members and H-1B workers are not treated as a single category. Economic development abroad, lawful immigration sponsorship, lobbying, litigation, donations and civic participation are not wrongdoing.
The central accountability question is whether Texas made a promise it built no consistent public accounting system to measure.
The proposed reform is therefore concrete: a machine-readable contractor labor ledger linking the prime vendor, material subcontractor, labor category, Texas residency, actual work geography, aggregate work-authorization category, hours and dollars — with privacy protections for individuals.

Appendix C — Definitions
Term
	Definition used in this report
	Direct state employee
	A worker employed by a Texas public entity.
	Contractor employee
	A worker employed by a private company performing work for a public customer.
	Subcontractor
	A lower-tier private company or worker used by a prime vendor under the public contract.
	ITSAC
	Texas DIR's IT Staff Augmentation Contracting vehicle for acquiring individual technology labor categories.
	DBITS
	DIR Deliverables-Based IT Services; services are organized around deliverables rather than simply staff hours.
	LCA
	Labor Condition Application filed with the U.S. Department of Labor in connection with H-1B and related specialty-worker processes.
	H-1B-dependent employer
	A statutory/regulatory employer classification based on the proportion of H-1B workers; it does not mean every worker is H-1B.
	APNRTS
	Andhra Pradesh Non-Resident Telugu Society, a government-linked organization serving diaspora/NRT functions including investment facilitation.
	TxEDC
	Texas Economic Development Corporation, the private nonprofit side of Texas's public-private economic-development partnership.
	TEF
	Texas Enterprise Fund, a state incentive program administered through the state economic-development apparatus, not by TxEDC itself.
